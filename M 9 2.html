LESSON 2.1

Revision on Natural Numbers and Integers

1. Natural Numbers (ℕ)

Natural numbers are the counting numbers used to count objects.

ℕ = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, ...}
Example: 1, 5, 10, 25 and 100 are natural numbers.

2. Integers (ℤ)

Integers include positive whole numbers, negative whole numbers, and zero.

ℤ = {..., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...}
Example: -15, -2, 0, 7 and 25 are integers.
Every natural number is an integer, but not every integer is a natural number.
ℕ ⊂ ℤ

Examples

Classify the following numbers: 3, -2, 11, 0, -18, 15.

2.1.1 Euclid's Division Lemma

When one non-negative integer is divided by a positive integer, we get a quotient and a remainder.

a = b × q + r

Where:

  • a: Dividend
  • b: Divisor
  • q: Quotient
  • r: Remainder
0 ≤ r < b
Example: Divide 38 by 4.

38 ÷ 4 = 9 remainder 2.

38 = 4 × 9 + 2

Dividend = 38, divisor = 4, quotient = 9, remainder = 2.

Another example: Divide 5 by 14.

Since 5 is smaller than 14:

5 = 14 × 0 + 5

Quotient = 0 and remainder = 5.

A number is divisible by another number if the remainder is zero.

2.1.2 Prime and Composite Numbers

Factors and Multiples

A factor divides a number exactly. A multiple is obtained by multiplying a number by a natural number.

Example: 3 × 4 = 12.
  • 3 and 4 are factors of 12.
  • 12 is a multiple of 3 and 4.

Prime Numbers

A natural number with exactly two distinct factors, 1 and itself, is called a prime number.

Examples: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17

Composite Numbers

A natural number with more than two factors is called a composite number.

Examples: 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 15
Remember:
  • 1 is neither prime nor composite.
  • 2 is the only even prime number.
  • Not every odd number is prime.
Example: Is 18 prime or composite?

Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18.

It has more than two factors, so 18 is composite.

Prime Factorization

Prime factorization is expressing a composite number as a product of prime numbers.

Example: Express 72 as prime factors.

72 = 2 × 36

= 2 × 2 × 18

= 2 × 2 × 2 × 9

= 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3

72 = 2³ × 3²

Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

Every composite number can be expressed as a product of primes, and this factorization is unique apart from the order of the prime factors.

2.1.3 Divisibility Tests

Divisibility tests help us check whether a number is divisible by another number without performing long division.

DivisorDivisibility rule
2Last digit is 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8.
3Sum of digits is divisible by 3.
4Last two digits form a number divisible by 4.
5Last digit is 0 or 5.
6Divisible by both 2 and 3.
8Last three digits form a number divisible by 8.
9Sum of digits is divisible by 9.
10Last digit is 0.
Example: Is 765 divisible by 3?

Sum of digits = 7 + 6 + 5 = 18.

18 is divisible by 3.

Therefore, 765 is divisible by 3.
Example: Is 384 divisible by 8?

384 ÷ 8 = 48.

Therefore, 384 is divisible by 8.

2.1.4 Greatest Common Factor and Least Common Multiple

Greatest Common Factor (GCF)

The GCF of two or more natural numbers is the greatest number that is a factor of all the given numbers.

Example: Find GCF(12, 18).

Factors of 12 = 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12.

Factors of 18 = 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18.

Common factors = 1, 2, 3, 6.

GCF(12, 18) = 6

GCF using Prime Factorization

Write each number as a product of prime factors. Multiply the common prime factors using their smallest powers.

Example:

12 = 2² × 3

18 = 2 × 3²

Common factors with smallest powers = 2 × 3.

GCF = 2 × 3 = 6

Least Common Multiple (LCM)

The LCM of two or more natural numbers is the smallest positive number that is a multiple of all the given numbers.

Example: Find LCM(6, 9).

Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, ...

Multiples of 9: 9, 18, 27, 36, ...

LCM(6, 9) = 18

LCM using Prime Factorization

Take every prime factor using its greatest power.

6 = 2 × 3

9 = 3²

LCM = 2 × 3² = 18
Important relationship:
GCF(a,b) × LCM(a,b) = a × b

Example: 6 × 18 = 6 × 18 = 108 for the numbers 6 and 18.

LESSON 2.2

Rational Numbers

A rational number is any number that can be written in the form of a fraction where the numerator and denominator are integers and the denominator is not zero.

a/b, where a,b ∈ ℤ and b ≠ 0

The set of rational numbers is represented by ℚ.

Examples:
  • 1/2
  • -3/4
  • 5 = 5/1
  • 0 = 0/1
  • 0.25 = 1/4
Every integer is a rational number because it can be written over 1.
ℤ ⊂ ℚ

2.2.1 Representation of Rational Numbers by Decimals

We can convert a rational number into decimal form by dividing the numerator by the denominator.

Terminating Decimals

A decimal is terminating if the division process ends with a remainder of zero.

Example:
3/5 = 0.6

The decimal ends, so it is terminating.

Repeating Decimals

A repeating decimal has one or more digits that repeat forever.

Examples:
1/3 = 0.3333... = 0.3̅
23/99 = 0.232323... = 0.2̅3̅

Converting Terminating Decimals into Fractions

Example 1: Convert 7.3 into a fraction.

There is one digit after the decimal point, so multiply by 10.

7.3 = 73/10
Example 2: Convert -0.18 into a fraction.
-0.18 = -18/100 = -9/50

Locating Rational Numbers on a Number Line

Every rational number can be represented as a point on the number line.

Example: Locate -2.5, 0, 1.5 and 3.

Arrange them from smallest to largest:

-2.5 < 0 < 1.5 < 3

2.2.2 Conversion of Repeating Decimals into Fractions

We can convert a repeating decimal into a fraction by multiplying and subtracting to eliminate the repeating part.

Example: Convert 0.555... into a fraction.

Let x = 0.555...

Multiply both sides by 10:

10x = 5.555...

Subtract the first equation:

10x - x = 5.555... - 0.555...

9x = 5

x = 5/9
Another example: Convert 0.121212... into a fraction.

Let x = 0.121212...

100x = 12.121212...

100x - x = 12

99x = 12

x = 12/99 = 4/33
If two digits repeat, multiply by 100. If three digits repeat, multiply by 1000.
LESSON 2.3

Irrational Numbers

Perfect Squares

A perfect square is a number that can be expressed as the product of two equal integers.

1 = 1², 4 = 2², 9 = 3², 16 = 4²

Square Roots

A square root of a number x is a number y such that y² = x.

Example:

√25 = 5 because 5² = 25.

Both 5 and -5 are square roots of 25, but √25 represents the principal square root, which is 5.

What is an Irrational Number?

A decimal number that is neither terminating nor repeating is called an irrational number.

Examples: √2, √3, √5, π
Example: Determine whether √25 is rational or irrational.

√25 = 5.

5 can be written as 5/1.

Therefore, √25 is rational.
Example: Is √7 rational or irrational?

7 is not a perfect square, so √7 is irrational.

A square root of a positive integer that is not a perfect square is irrational.

Comparing Square Roots

For positive numbers, if a is less than b, then √a is less than √b.

If 0 < a < b, then √a < √b
Example: Compare √5 and √7.

Since 5 is less than 7:

√5 < √7

Locating Irrational Numbers on the Number Line

Example: Locate √2.

Since 1² = 1 and 2² = 4:

1 < √2 < 2

Using a calculator, √2 ≈ 1.4142.

Therefore, √2 lies between 1 and 2.

2.3.2 Operations on Irrational Numbers

Multiplication of Radicals

√a × √b = √(ab)
Example:

√2 × √3 = √6

√5 × √5 = 5

Division of Radicals

√a / √b = √(a/b), where a,b > 0
Example:

√18 / √2 = √9 = 3

Converting to a Single Radical

a√b = √(a²b), for a ≥ 0
Example:

3√2 = √(9 × 2) = √18

Addition and Subtraction

We can combine like radicals by adding or subtracting their coefficients.

Example 1:
2√3 + 4√3 = 6√3
Example 2:
5√2 - 2√2 = 3√2

Simplifying Radicals

Example: Simplify √8 + √2.

√8 = √(4 × 2) = 2√2

2√2 + √2 = 3√2

Answer = 3√2

Rationalizing a Denominator

Rationalization is the process of changing a fraction so that its denominator becomes rational.

Example: Rationalize 1/√2.

Multiply the numerator and denominator by √2.

1/√2 × √2/√2 = √2/2
LESSON 2.4

Real Numbers

A real number is a number that is either rational or irrational.

ℝ = ℚ ∪ {irrational numbers}

Classification of Numbers

  • Natural numbers: 1, 2, 3, ...
  • Integers: ..., -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, ...
  • Rational numbers: 1/2, -3/4, 0.25
  • Irrational numbers: √2, √3, π
  • Real numbers: all rational and irrational numbers.

Comparing Real Numbers

For two real numbers a and b, exactly one of the following is true:

a < b, or a = b, or a > b

This is called the trichotomy property.

Transitive Property

If a < b and b < c, then a < c.
Example: Compare -8 and -2.

On the number line, -8 is to the left of -2.

-8 < -2

Finding a Real Number Between Two Numbers

One method is to calculate their average.

Average = (a + b) / 2
Example: Find a real number between 2 and 4.2.

(2 + 4.2) / 2 = 3.1

2 < 3.1 < 4.2

There are infinitely many real numbers between any two distinct real numbers.

2.4.1 Intervals

A real interval is a set containing all real numbers between two given numbers, with the endpoints included or excluded as specified.

Types of Intervals

NotationMeaning
[a,b]Includes both endpoints.
(a,b)Excludes both endpoints.
[a,b)Includes a, excludes b.
(a,b]Excludes a, includes b.
(-∞,b)All real numbers less than b.
[a,∞)All real numbers greater than or equal to a.
Remember:
  • [ ] means the endpoint is included.
  • ( ) means the endpoint is excluded.
  • Infinity always uses parentheses.
Example 1: Real numbers between 3 and 5 including both endpoints.
[3,5]
Example 2: Numbers between -2 and 2 including -2 but excluding 2.
[-2,2)
Example 3: Numbers less than 2, excluding 2.
(-∞,2)
Example 4: Numbers greater than or equal to zero.
[0,∞)

2.4.2 Absolute Values

The absolute value of a real number is its distance from zero on the number line.

|x| = x, if x ≥ 0
|x| = -x, if x < 0
Examples:

|5| = 5

|-5| = 5

|0| = 0

|-0.4| = 0.4

Distance Between Two Numbers

Distance = |a - b|
Example: Find the distance between -3 and 4.

|-3 - 4| = |-7| = 7

Distance = 7 units
Absolute value is never negative.

For example, |x| = -4 has no real solution.

2.4.3 Exponents and Radicals

Exponents

An exponential expression represents repeated multiplication of the same number.

aⁿ = a × a × a × ... × a

Here, a is the base and n is the exponent or power.

Examples:

2³ = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8

(-3)² = (-3) × (-3) = 9

3⁴ = 81

Zero Exponent

a⁰ = 1, where a ≠ 0

Negative Exponent

a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ, where a ≠ 0
Example:

2⁻³ = 1/2³ = 1/8

Roots

  • Square root: If a² = b, then a is a square root of b.
  • Cube root: If a³ = b, then a is a cube root of b.
  • nth root: If aⁿ = b, then a is an nth root of b.

Principal nth Root

The principal nth root is written using the radical symbol.

ⁿ√b

The radical symbol is √, n is the index, and b is the radicand.

Examples:

√9 = 3

³√8 = 2

⁴√81 = 3

⁵√(-32) = -2

Fractional Exponents

a^(1/n) = ⁿ√a

9^(1/2) = √9 = 3

8^(1/3) = ³√8 = 2

Laws of Exponents

LawFormula
Multiplicationaⁿ × aᵐ = aⁿ⁺ᵐ
Divisionaⁿ / aᵐ = aⁿ⁻ᵐ
Power of a power(aⁿ)ᵐ = aⁿᵐ
Power of a product(ab)ⁿ = aⁿbⁿ
Power of a quotient(a/b)ⁿ = aⁿ/bⁿ

For the division law, a ≠ 0. For the quotient law, b ≠ 0.

Example 1: Simplify 2³ × 2².

= 2³⁺²

= 2⁵

= 32
Example 2: Simplify (3²)³.

= 3²ˣ³

= 3⁶ = 729
Example 3: Simplify √50.

= √(25 × 2)

= √25 × √2

= 5√2
LESSON 2.5

Applications

We use the concepts learned in this unit to solve practical problems.

Application 1: Euclid's Division Lemma

Problem: A gardener has 530 flower pots. Each row must contain 21 pots. How many complete rows can be made, and how many pots remain?

530 ÷ 21 = 25 remainder 5.

530 = 21 × 25 + 5

Answer: 25 complete rows and 5 remaining pots.

Application 2: GCF

Problem: A board measures 50 cm by 20 cm. What is the largest possible side length of equal square pieces that can cover it completely, using natural-number lengths?

We need GCF(50,20).

50 = 2 × 5²

20 = 2² × 5

GCF = 2 × 5 = 10.

Answer: 10 cm

Application 3: LCM

Problem: Two students complete one round of a sports field in 6 minutes and 14 minutes. After how many minutes will they meet at the starting point again?

Find LCM(6,14).

6 = 2 × 3

14 = 2 × 7

LCM = 2 × 3 × 7 = 42.

Answer: 42 minutes
